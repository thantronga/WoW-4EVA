# Post-Panel Interviews 2026-09-14 — WoW Forever

What surfaced **outside the panels** at and after BlizzCon 2026: developer interviews, show-floor
hands-on reporting, and one official Blizzard article this vault had not fully mined.

Sibling captures: **[[Deep Dive Panel 2026-09-14]]** (the Sunday systems panel) and
**[[Realms and Rulesets]]** (the separate Hardcore panel). This note deliberately does not repeat
either.

> [!warning] Tier discipline for this note
> **Interview content is Tier 2, not Tier 1.** A developer quoted directly by an outlet is still a
> third party transcribing speech — the same rule this vault applies to panel slides. Only the
> Blizzard-authored article in the last section is Tier 1.
>
> Hands-on demo reporting is **Tier 3** — a journalist reading a show-floor build, which is the
> same class of evidence as the talent extraction and carries the same warning: the demo build is

## The headline: Blizzard has stated the timeline placement

> [!success] Tier 1 — and it closes an open question the vault had explicitly refused to close
> Blizzard's own article **["Carve a New Path with World of Warcraft: Forever"](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24302093/carve-a-new-path-with-world-of-warcraft-forever)**
> (news **24302093**, published pre-BlizzCon) states it outright:
>
> > "Set after the events of *Warcraft III Reforged: Forsaken Kingdom* and before Molten Core,
> > players will return to the original continents, adventure to level 60, and continue
> > progressing through new stories, experiences, and updates…"
>
> [[Open Questions]] carried this placement as *"a wiki editor's placement, not a Blizzard
> statement — **low authority, do not quote as fact**"*, and the 14 Sep pass, having closed the
> *separate-continuity* question from the Found Photographs panel, left the **chronological
> placement inside that continuity** explicitly still open.
>
> **It is Blizzard's own wording, in a Blizzard-authored article. Tier 1.** The Warcraft Wiki
> editor was reading Blizzard's copy, not inventing a placement.

**The meta-lesson, and it is now the third instance in three days.** The vault has three times
scored something as unverified or low-authority while the answer sat in an official Blizzard
article — the Skyborne racials (13 Sep), Ion Hazzikostas's presenter role (14 Sep), and now the
timeline. In all three the correction came from **re-reading a source already cited**, not from
finding a new one. Before scoring anything "unverified", re-read the official articles.

## Developer interviews

### Anna Resendez and Clayton Stone — But Why Tho, Forever-specific

**[Tier 2]** The most substantive Forever interview published so far, and the only one carrying
mechanics that appear in **no** panel recap.

**Two genuinely new facts:**

- **The new raids do not require attunement at launch**, mirroring the original entry-level
  raids. Nothing in either panel recap mentions attunement.
- **Raid sizes are fixed. There is no flex scaling.** Forever launches with 10- and 20-player
  raids at those exact sizes. Anna Resendez: *"We don't wanna lean towards content tuning or
  automatically changing the difficulty… it's always gonna stay the same."*

**Design reasoning:**

- **Deliberately conservative relative to Season of Discovery.** Clayton Stone: *"We are starting
  a little bit on a more conservative side. We certainly heard feedback from players that with
  Season of Discovery, we pushed the envelope about as far as we could push it."* This is a
  sharper framing than [[Design Pillars]] currently carries, where SoD is described as the
  experiment that established how far the team *could* push. The interview says they concluded
  it pushed **too** far.
- **SoD ideas will be folded in gradually, not wholesale.** Stone: *"We will certainly bring in
  more and more of those interesting things… for our classes."*
- **Encounter design favours accessibility over mechanical complexity** — Resendez framed
  encounters as avoiding complex mechanics and prioritising group play over difficulty scaling.
  Consistent with the Deep Dive's *"deliberate, tactical, and class-driven"* line.

> [!note] Name spellings differ from the official recaps
> This outlet writes **"Anna Resendez"** and **"Clayton Stone"**; Blizzard's recaps write **"Ana
> Resendez"** and **"Clay Stone"**. Almost certainly the same two people with the outlet using
> full forms. Recorded so a future search on either spelling finds this note. Note this is
> *separate* from the unresolved **Nora Mills vs Ana Resendez** title clash in
> [[Deep Dive Panel 2026-09-14]].

### Ion Hazzikostas and Holly Longdale — But Why Tho, Midnight interview touching Forever

**[Tier 2]** Primarily a retail Midnight interview. **Only the Forever-relevant parts are recorded
here** — see the contamination register in [[Sources and Confidence]] before mining this source
further.

- **Updates will roll out region by region, not simultaneously.** Hazzikostas describes
  *"staggered rollouts of our updates… across regions"*, reasoning that a global-simultaneous
  patch would put European servers *"offline from like 5 p.m. to 11 p.m."* **This matters for
  Australian players specifically** — a staggered model means the APAC window is a separate
  decision from the Americas window, and content unlock times may not track the launch-time
  conversion in [[Roadmap]].
- **The lighting work is art, not just an engine upgrade.** Hazzikostas: *"the lighting tech…
  isn't just an engine improvement. It's also work… by our environment artists across the
  entirety of those zones to relight them."* Duskwood is the example given — players carrying
  torches and lanterns casting real-time illumination, over classic assets. [[Systems]] lists the
  rendering updates but frames them as engine work; this reframes the effort.
- **Some Forever technology may migrate to retail** — improved server infrastructure, the
  lighting, and *"maybe even surnames, depending on how that plays out and how players feel about
  that in Forever."* Hazzikostas cautions it is *"not as simple as just flipping a switch."*
  Worth noting only because it makes surnames a **deliberate experiment** rather than a fix for
  a realmless world's naming problem.

### Ion Hazzikostas — Men's Journal

**[Tier 2]** Vision and positioning; no new mechanics.

- The ambition, in his words: *"take the best MMO ever made and make it better."*
- **Three target audiences** — players who loved Classic and want more of it; players who found
  Classic *"too crusty, too janky"*; and lapsed Classic players wanting something fresh inside
  that framework.
- On longevity: **"There's no expiration date here, and that's why we called it what we did."**
  The clearest statement yet of what the name is meant to signal, and a useful companion to Holly
  Longdale's *"a new beginning that never ends"* already in [[Design Pillars]].
- **Native gamepad support** is framed as an **accessibility** feature — for players intimidated
  by keyboard-and-mouse — not as a console-port signal.

## Show-floor hands-on

### The demo build — Blizzard Watch, 12 Sep

**[Tier 3 — demo-read]** Published the day *before* the Deep Dive, so this vault's Deep Dive
capture did not draw on it.

- **Character creation already required a two-part name.** This is the interesting one: the
  surname requirement was visible on the show floor a **full day before** the realmless
  architecture was announced as the reason for it. **Retroactive corroboration** of the
  [[Realms and Rulesets|realmless]] reveal from an independent, earlier observation.
- **Edit Mode UI customisation, comparable to modern WoW.** Not currently in
  [[Systems|Systems § Presentation]], which lists the HD/SD toggle, visual presets and gamepad
  support but not Edit Mode.
- **Class trainers are still required** to learn abilities after levelling.
- **Starting quests are presented in groups with map tracking**, rather than Classic's one-at-a-
  time introduction.
- **Mana management is Classic-like** — the reporter ran dry at level 1.
- Xbox controller support described as **alpha**.
- The demo covered gameplay only to **level 3**, which is a much shorter slice than the level-38
  characters the talent extraction used. Different demo stations, different builds.

### The Legacy system hands-on — Blizzard Watch, 12 Sep

**[Tier 3 — demo-read]** Adds mechanism the official recap does not.

- The framing: *"a mash up of Achievements, a skill tree, and a seasonal"* journey.
- **Only certain meta-level challenges award Legacy Points.** Legacy Challenges behave like
  ordinary achievements across professions, dungeons and raids, but **most do not pay out** —
  the points come from meta-challenges above them. Neither the official recap nor
  [[Talent System]]'s demo reading captures this, and it changes how the 65-point total should
  be read: 65 is not "65 achievements".
- **The Progress Track is keyed to points *spent*, and rewards are claimed from in-game NPCs.**
  Only **four** rewards were visible at preview.
- Tree reward examples read on the floor: increased chance to receive a **skill increase**,
  extended **rested experience**, and **inventory improvements**.

> [!warning] Conflict — what unlocks the Legacy Reward Track?
> The **official** recap says accumulation **beyond the 16-point spend cap** unlocks the Legacy
> Reward Track. **Blizzard Watch's hands-on** says the Progress Track pays out on **total points
> spent**. Those are different triggers — one keys off the overflow you cannot spend, the other
> off the spending itself.
>
> **Prefer the official wording** for now, but this is a mechanism the vault should nail down at
> beta rather than leave as two paraphrases. Recorded in [[Systems]] and [[Open Questions]].

### Gamepad support hands-on — DayOne, 13 Sep

**[Tier 3 — demo-read]** [[Systems]] records "official gamepad support" as a single line. What it
actually is:

- **Toggled in the menus**, which converts the whole interface to controller-compatible form.
- **Radial selection menus.**
- Binding layers: abilities on the **d-pad** at base, then on face and d-pad buttons while
  holding **RT**, **LT**, or **both** — reported as **"over 20 places to bind abilities."**
- **Targeting: LB for friendlies, RB for enemies.** Tap to cycle; **hold LB and use the d-pad** to
  select a specific party member directly. Clear visual markers on the current target, with the
  party frame as the reference — the reporter found healing workable on a pad.
- **Limitation:** ground-targeted AoE such as *Blizzard* centres on the **current target**. There
  is no manual placement with the right stick.
- Described as **alpha** at launch, **built for Forever**, with retail implementation unconfirmed
  — which matches the Hazzikostas interview above.

## A source change: Wowhead's talent calculator is no longer a placeholder

> [!important] Retire a contamination-register entry — 13 Sep 2026
> [[Sources and Confidence]] and [[Open Questions]] both carry a **DO NOT FILE** warning that
> Wowhead's Forever talent endpoint is **byte-for-byte identical to Classic Era** and therefore a
> placeholder. **That was accurate when checked and is now out of date.**
>
> Wowhead published **["WoW: Forever Talent Calculator Now Live"](https://www.wowhead.com/forever/news/wow-forever-talent-calculator-now-live-382870)**
> (article **382870**, 13 Sep) announcing that the calculator now carries **real Forever data**
> drawn from **the BlizzCon panels and testing sessions**.
>
> **The new caveat is Wowhead's own:** the data is *"mostly"* accurate, but **"talent rank scaling
> is potentially wrong"** because of time constraints, and some new talents are missing icons.
> Wowhead commits to rebuilding it by **datamining once the beta client is available**.

**How to score it now.** Wowhead's calculator is **Tier 2 for talent names and positions** and
**explicitly unreliable for rank values** — which is the *same* weakness as the community
extraction in [[Sources and Confidence|Tier 3]], arrived at independently. Two separate projects
now say the identical thing: **names and rank 1 are usable, ranks 2+ are not.** That agreement is
itself mildly reassuring about the names.

**What has not changed:** anyone citing an *older* snapshot of Wowhead's Forever calculator is
still citing Classic Era. The warning is retired, not reversed.

## Bracket and range cross-checks

**Blizzard Watch published a zones/dungeons/raids round-up on 13 Sep** that lists all nine dungeon
brackets. **They match [[Dungeons]] exactly**, including the two figures the 13 Sep pass corrected
against MMO-Champion — **Krol'dok Stronghold 40–55** and **Blackmaw Hold 55–60**. That is a
further independent confirmation of the corrected table.

Two small divergences in the same article:

- **Riverglades is given as 30–45.** That is a **fourth** distinct reading, against the three
  already in [[Zones]] (mid-30s–mid-40s / 35–55 / ~40–55). It is the **lowest ceiling** proposed
  so far and sits awkwardly with a 40–55 dungeon inside the zone. Recorded, not adopted.
- **City of Dalaran's location is given as "Alterac Valley."** [[Dungeons]] says **Alterac
  Mountains**. Alterac Valley is the battleground; the Mountains are the zone. **Treat this as the
  outlet's slip and keep Alterac Mountains.**

## One official article contradicts itself on the launch time

**[Tier 1, both sides]** Article **24302093** gives the launch as *"November 4 at 3:00 p.m. PDT
globally"* in one place and *"November 4, 2026, at 3:00 p.m. PST"* in another — **within the same
article**. The pre-purchase article (**24301508**) says **PST**.

**[[Roadmap]]'s reading is correct and stands: PST.** US daylight time ends **1 November 2026**,
so 4 November is unambiguously PST, and two of the three official mentions say so. The stakes are
one hour on the Sydney conversion — 3pm PST is **10:00 Thu 5 Nov AEDT**; 3pm PDT would be 09:00.
Flagged in [[Roadmap]] so nobody re-derives it from the wrong article.
