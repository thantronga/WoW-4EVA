# Realms and Rulesets — WoW Forever

> [!info] Where the realmless system is documented
> The full specification of the **realmless** model — the four rulesets, cross-ruleset rules,
> two-part names, language preference, faction balancing — lives in
> **[[Systems|Systems § Realmless Azeroth]]**. It is not repeated here.
>
> This note covers the **Hardcore ruleset** in depth, and captures the **separate Hardcore
> panel** that aired on 13 Sep and was still logged as "unestablished" elsewhere in this vault.

---

## The short version

**[Tier 1]** Forever has **no realms**. You choose a **ruleset** — **Normal**, **PvP**,
**Roleplaying**, or **Hardcore** — and each is a large ecosystem rather than a server. Grouping
is within your own ruleset and your own faction. Collections and Legacy Points are account-wide
across the three regular rulesets. Characters get **two-part names**, unique per region.

This **overturns** what the vault previously recorded about Hardcore running on separate official
realms — see the correction in [[Roadmap]] and in [[Systems]].

---

## Hardcore as a ruleset

**[Tier 1]**

- **Permanent death.**
- **Available after launch**, not at launch. **[Tier 2 — Output Lag]** also **not in the initial
  beta build** on 17 Sep, and **no date was given** at the Hardcore panel. The [[Roadmap]] still
  carries a **Winter 2026/27** season label from the roadmap slide — that remains the only timing
  Blizzard has published.
- The ecosystem is **fully enclosed**. No items, mounts, pets or Legacy Points enter from outside.
- **Progression flows one way, outward.** What you earn in Hardcore is shared to your other
  rulesets; nothing flows back in. Blizzard Watch's gloss: *"you have to earn it."*
- **On death, a Hardcore character can transfer to another ruleset — including PvP**, which
  Blizzard Watch flags as new versus WoW Classic.

> [!note] The realm-count question is void, not answered
> "How many Hardcore realms will there be" has no answer because there are no realms. The
> **ruleset** half of that old open question is now fully answered; the count half is retired.

## The Hardcore panel — 13 Sep, 4:15 pm PDT

> [!important] This panel did air, and it carried material the Deep Dive did not
> [[Open Questions]] and [[Index]] previously recorded that a separate Hardcore panel was
> *referenced* in the Out of Games live blog but that **whether it aired, and whether it added
> anything, was unestablished**. It is now established.
>
> Blizzard's own BlizzCon schedule lists **"World of Warcraft Hardcore: What's Next"** at
> **4:15 pm PDT Sunday 13 Sep = 09:15 Mon 14 Sep AEST** — about **90 minutes before this note was
> written**. Three items below come from it and appear nowhere in the Deep Dive coverage.

> [!warning] Treat this section as provisional — single-source and very fresh
> **Blizzard published no recap for the Hardcore panel**, and **Output Lag is effectively the only
> outlet with post-panel material** this early, published as short single-topic pieces while its
> own live post was still serving a pre-panel placeholder. Everything here is **Tier 2,
> single-source**. Re-check once other outlets publish — this is the weakest-sourced block in the
> vault's 14 Sep additions.

**[Tier 2 — Output Lag]**

### The world-refresh warning — the most consequential item

When player density in an area gets too high or too low, the game rebuilds that area and respawns
creatures around you. In Classic Hardcore this is a way to die with no warning and no mistake.
Forever adds a **three-minute countdown window** with a **"move now" button** that lets you
confirm you are somewhere safe and trigger the refresh immediately rather than waiting it out.

Principal Game Designer **Kris Zierhut**: *"no more are you going to lose your characters to this
sudden change of the world around you."*

Two details that make this bigger than a Hardcore feature: it was **built for Hardcore but ships
in the whole game**, and it is **testable in the beta from 17 September** — unlike Hardcore
itself. It is therefore one of the few Hardcore-panel claims that can be verified within days.

### Hardcore-specific Legacy challenges

Hardcore gets **its own Legacy challenges** when it launches. Zierhut: *"there are a bunch of
hardcore specific legacy challenges to do,"* with more possible later. The standard PvP challenges
do not apply there.

**A death after level 25 still earns a Legacy point.** Zierhut frames it as *"a softer landing
after a Hardcore death"* — the next run starts with slightly more rested experience and gathering.
The Legacy system itself is in [[Systems]]; its demo-build reading is in [[Talent System]].

### Mak'gora — a tease, not an announcement

Senior Game Designer **Josh Greenfield** called Mak'gora *"a, I would say, unexpectedly popular
part of Hardcore"* and floated a venue for it:

> "Something that has been asked of us for a really long time is like, what if we had a dedicated
> space for Mak'gora contests? Could be kind of cool. Could be something we're working on for a
> future update. Maybe."

**No date, no mechanics, no mockup, and it was off-script.** File it as a tease. In Classic
Hardcore, Mak'gora is a permanent-death duel — the loser's character is gone.

## Still unknown

- **The mechanism** behind the ruleset ecosystems — channels, layering, sharding. Blizzard
  described the player-facing model only.
- **Hardcore's date.** A season label and "after launch". Nothing firmer.
- **What "unique per region" implies** for a world with no realms. Names are region-scoped, so
  regions remain a boundary of some kind; nobody asked what kind. **[14 Sep, related]**
  Hazzikostas separately describes **staggered update rollouts across regions**, so regions are a
  scheduling boundary too, not only a naming one. See [[Post-Panel Interviews 2026-09-14]].

> [!note] Two-part names were visible on the show floor before the reveal
> **[Tier 3]** Blizzard Watch's demo session on **12 Sep** — a day before the realmless
> announcement — already required a **two-part name** at character creation. Independent, earlier
> corroboration that surnames are real and not a slide-only concept.
>
> **[Tier 2]** Hazzikostas has since floated bringing surnames to **retail**, *"depending on how
> that plays out and how players feel about that in Forever"* — which frames them as a deliberate
> experiment, not merely a fix for a realmless world's naming problem.
- **Whether other outlets corroborate the Hardcore panel content above.** As of writing, none had
  published.
