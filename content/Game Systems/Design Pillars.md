# Design Pillars — WoW Forever

[CONFIRMED] Four pillars, presented by **Tim Jones, Lead Classic Designer**.

**1. Approachable and familiar.**
Every addition must feel like it could have existed in Classic. Explicitly: **no flying
mounts, no level scaling.**

**2. The world as the main character.**
Levelling matters as much as endgame. Stories are often small and local rather than
world-saving.

**3. Journey before the destination.**
Level **60 cap forever** — no planned cap increases. The only catch-up mechanism is playing
the game.

**4. Protect and nurture social experiences.**
Deliberate friction. Every quality-of-life choice is judged by how it shapes the way players
meet each other.

## Positioning

Forever is a **third pillar alongside Modern and Classic**. Not a mode, not a season, not a
new version of Classic — a permanent parallel product.

**Season of Discovery** is framed as the experiment that established how far the team could
push before it stopped feeling like Classic.

> [!important] The interviews sharpen this — 14 Sep 2026
> **[Tier 2]** Clayton Stone, to But Why Tho after the panels: *"We are starting a little bit on
> a more conservative side. We certainly heard feedback from players that with Season of
> Discovery, we **pushed the envelope about as far as we could push it**."*
>
> That is a stronger claim than the framing above. On stage SoD was the experiment that mapped
> the limit; in the interview it is the experiment that found the limit by **overshooting**, and
> Forever is deliberately positioned back from it. Stone adds that SoD ideas will be folded in
> **gradually** — *"We will certainly bring in more and more of those interesting things… for our
> classes"* — rather than imported wholesale.
>
> This is the reasoning behind several otherwise-unexplained decisions: fixed raid sizes with no
> flex ([[Raids]]), simpler encounter mechanics, and the "conservative at launch" shape of the
> class changes. See [[Post-Panel Interviews 2026-09-14]].

Holly Longdale: *"a new permanent home, a new beginning that never ends."*

**The critique of retail embedded in the pitch:** matchmaking systems reduced player
interaction to bare-bones functional basics in the pursuit of maximum efficiency. This is the
stated justification for pillar 4 and for systems like [[Systems|Camping]].

## Continuity — answered 14 Sep

> [!important] This closes a standing open question, but **not** from the Deep Dive panel
> [[Open Questions]] listed *"whether Forever sits in a separate timeline from current WoW"* as
> open, noting that Warcraft Wiki's chronological placement was a wiki editor's inference with no
> Blizzard statement behind it.
>
> **Blizzard has now said it.** Senior Game Designer **Josh Greenfield** stated that Forever
> *"does not exist in the same continuity as modern World of Warcraft"*. **Chris Metzen**'s term
> for it is **"our little time bubble."**
>
> **Source caveat:** this came from the **"World of Warcraft: Forever Found Photographs"** panel,
> **not** the Deep Dive, and is carried by **Output Lag alone** among the outlets checked. A
> direct developer quote, so **Tier 1 on content** — but **single-outlet on transcription**.
> Confirm the wording if a second source or an official recap appears.

**The stated constraint on that freedom.** The team wants room to tell its own stories *"without
creating problematic situations that require a direct response in the modern game"*, while
refusing to rewrite foundational lore — they intend to *"paint inside the lines of established
foundational lore as much as possible."* Greenfield's example of what will **not** happen:

> "We aren't going to suddenly say, in this universe, Jaina was a dread lord all along, or Arthas
> never became the Lich King."

**What this settles and what it does not.** It settles that Forever is a **separate continuity**,
which is the part that was open. It gives **no chronological placement** — the article does not
state one. **Warcraft Wiki's "after the Forsaken Kingdom campaign, before Molten Core" placement
remains an unsourced editor inference and should still not be quoted as fact.**

## Notable quotes

Clay Stone, on Camping's purpose — people might *"just maybe, talk to each other."*

Clay Stone, on future content — **"nothing is off the table, not even a new class."**

Ion Hazzikostas, on what the name is meant to signal — **"There's no expiration date here, and
that's why we called it what we did."** **[Tier 2]**, Men's Journal. Reads as the companion to
Holly Longdale's *"a new beginning that never ends"* above.

Ion Hazzikostas, on the ambition — *"take the best MMO ever made and make it better."*
**[Tier 2]**. He frames the audience as three groups: players who loved Classic and want more,
players who found Classic *"too crusty, too janky"*, and lapsed Classic players wanting something
fresh inside that framework.
(Note: this is an openness statement, not an announcement. There is **no** new class in
Forever — the class count is nine. See [[Sources and Confidence]] on the fabricated
new-specs leak.)
